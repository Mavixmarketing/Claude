# Version 5: what the writers now do (18 Oct 2026)

Written for Farhan. This covers the changes made after your long message on 18 Oct. It applies to the email replies, the email follow-ups, the LinkedIn replies and the LinkedIn follow-ups.

## Price

- **Before the audit:** "Our price is personalised. It depends on how much work your site needs, what you need and your end goal. I set it after looking at your site, and it comes with the free audit." No numbers.
- **Only if they ask again or insist:** "Our plans run from $349 to $1,699 a month, and most businesses like yours start on Growth at $549, the plan with the money back promise."
- **After the audit:** Starter $349, Growth $549, Premium $1,699. Growth is always recommended first. Starter is mentioned only when money is clearly tight.
- **"Too expensive":** agree it's real money, never lower the price, compare it with what one extra customer is worth to them, and if money is tight after the audit, offer Starter or just the first fix. The plan can change, the number can't.
- Prices are always in US dollars.

## Money back

Growth comes with a 2 month money back promise and Premium with 3 months. If we miss the target we agreed, they get their money back plus $1,000 from us. Starter has none. The writers bring this up when people worry about risk, results or wasting money.

## Never say no

The writers never make anyone feel we can't handle it, that they aren't a good fit, or that it isn't worth it. Fully booked or happy with ads? Things change (ad costs go up, budgets get cut, the busy season ends), so having Google bring customers keeps them prepared. Only you decide who we don't work with. Risky businesses still get a normal answer, plus a flag to you.

## SEO compared with ads

SEO usually takes 3 to 6 months to build. Nobody can honestly promise a first place on Google. But once it works, it keeps bringing customers without paying for every click. Ads stop the day you stop paying. They work well together: ads for now, SEO for the long run.

## "Next year" or "later"

The writers agree, then give a solid reason that waiting costs them: SEO takes 3 to 6 months, so starting next year means results only late next year, while competitors who start now get there first. Then they offer to send the free audit now so it's ready, and save the date for a follow-up. Said once, confident, not pushy.

## Showing value with numbers

The writers use a rough, typical figure for what one customer is worth for that kind of business in that city, state or country, and compare it with the cost of SEO. The figure is always labelled as rough, and the maths must be right ($2,999 a month is $35,988 a year). Before the audit they compare value only, never our price.

## After the audit

- The first follow-up asks whether they've had a chance to look at the audit and whether anything in it raised a question, and names the one first thing we'd fix.
- Later follow-ups make the next step small: "Would you like me to send the scope and start steps for the Growth plan?"
- A "go" after the audit is not taken as a yes to paid work. The writer asks whether they want the scope and start steps.

## How we work (used when they ask, or when it helps them decide)

Their own business manager and SEO manager, a shared channel between their team and ours, a weekly update and a monthly meeting. Simple reports: before, after, and what it brought them. No long contract: our agreement has no time limit, and the only one it ties down is us, to deliver.

## Writing style

Like a real person: normal capital letters, contractions (I'll, we're), 40 to 80 words, very simple words. LinkedIn replies are shorter: 45 words at most, like someone typing on their phone.

## Video lessons added this time

- Answer what they asked first, then one next step.
- End with a specific decision question, not "does that make sense?".
- If the reason for a question isn't obvious, say why you're asking ("only asking because...").
- Never change the price to win a deal. Change the plan or the scope instead.
- Put the price next to the value of one extra customer.
- After the audit, ask whether anything in it raised a question (finds the real concern).
- Name one first step after the audit (one offer, one problem, one price).
- Don't turn replies into a free how-to lesson. Say what needs fixing, not how.
- Don't repeat the same reassurance twice.
