# 05 — The Sales Playbook

> *"the meeting part is difficult. I never prepare myself. Like, what should I speak? What should I do? What kind of shit should I speak onto the camera?"*

This file answers that. Word for word. Print it. Keep it open on a second screen during calls — nobody can tell.

**The core mindset shift:** a sales call is not a performance. You are not there to impress them or to talk. **You are there to diagnose.** A doctor doesn't walk in and pitch surgery — they ask questions until the problem is obvious, and then the treatment sells itself. If you are talking more than 30% of the call, you are doing it wrong.

That reframe alone will fix most of your anxiety, because you no longer need to know what to *say* — you only need to know what to *ask*. And the questions are all written below.

---

## Before the call — 10 minutes, non-negotiable

Never take a call cold. Ten minutes of prep is the difference between a closed deal and "let me think about it."

**Pull these 5 things and write them on one page:**

1. Their Google ranking for their #1 money keyword — and who's above them
2. Their Google Business Profile: rating, review count, last post, photo count
3. Their website: mobile speed, whether it has a clear call-to-action, whether it has location/service pages
4. Whether they're running Google Ads right now (search their brand + a money keyword)
5. **Their top competitor's numbers** — reviews, ranking, what they're doing differently

**Then write the three things you'll say.** Just three. That's your whole script beyond the questions:

> 1. "You're position 8 for '[keyword]' — [Competitor] is position 1."
> 2. "They have 87 reviews, you have 11."
> 3. "There are about 480 searches a month for that term in [City]."

That's it. Three facts. They will do more selling than anything you could say about yourself.

---

## Call structure — 30 minutes

| Phase | Time | Who talks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame | 2 min | You |
| 2. Discovery | 12 min | **Them (90%)** |
| 3. Gap + cost of inaction | 4 min | Both |
| 4. Prescription | 5 min | You |
| 5. Price | 2 min | You, then **silence** |
| 6. Objections | 3 min | Both |
| 7. Close + next step | 2 min | You |

---

### Phase 1 — Frame (2 min)

Say this almost verbatim. It sets control and removes the pressure from both of you.

> "Thanks for making time, [Name]. Here's how I'd like to use the next 30 minutes, if that works for you.
>
> First 10–15 minutes I'm just going to ask you questions about the business — where the customers come from now, what you've tried, what's working. I've done some homework on [Company] and I found a couple of things I want to show you, but I don't want to guess at what matters, so I'd rather hear it from you first.
>
> Then I'll tell you straight whether I think we can help. If I don't think we can, I'll say so and I'll point you somewhere better — I'd rather do that than take money for something that won't work.
>
> If I do think we can, I'll walk you through exactly what I'd do and what it costs, and you can tell me yes, no, or 'I need to think about it.' All three are fine.
>
> Sound good?"

**Why every part of this matters:**
- "I'll say so if we can't help" → immediately makes you the only honest person they've spoken to. Massive trust unlock.
- "yes, no, or think about it — all three are fine" → removes their guard, and quietly pre-authorises you to ask for a decision at the end.
- You've set the agenda → you control the call without being pushy.

---

### Phase 2 — Discovery (12 min) — THEY talk

**These are the only questions you need. Ask them, shut up, and take notes.**

Follow every answer with **"Tell me more about that"** or **"Why do you think that is?"** The second answer is always more useful than the first.

**The business:**
1. "Walk me through how a new customer finds you today — the most common path."
2. "Roughly how many new [customers/cases/jobs] do you get in a month right now?"
3. "What's a new [customer] worth to you on average? First job, or lifetime if you track it?" ← **the most important question on the call. Everything downstream is priced against this number.**
4. "Could you handle 10 more a month if they showed up tomorrow? Or would that break something?" ← qualifies capacity and reveals whether they're actually ready to buy

**The pain:**
5. "What made you take this call? What's going on right now that made marketing worth thinking about?"
6. "How long has that been a problem?"
7. "What have you already tried?" → then: **"What happened?"** ← this is where you learn who burned them and what you must not sound like
8. "What did that cost you?"

**The stakes:**
9. "If nothing changes over the next 12 months, what does that look like for the business?"
10. "Is that acceptable?" ← let the silence sit. This is the most powerful question in the whole script.

**Decision process:**
11. "If we did work together, who else is involved in that decision?"
12. "What would you need to see to feel confident this was the right call?"
13. "Have you got a budget in mind for this, or are we figuring that out together?"

**Take real notes.** Then read back: *"So what I'm hearing is [X, Y, Z]. Have I got that right?"* — this single move closes more deals than any pitch, because almost nobody has ever accurately repeated their problem back to them.

---

### Phase 3 — The gap (4 min)

Now, and only now, you show your three prepared facts. Do the arithmetic **out loud with them**, using *their* number from question 3.

> "So before the call I looked at a few things. Can I share what I found?
>
> You're position 8 for '[keyword]'. [Competitor] is position 1. That term gets about 480 searches a month in [City].
>
> Position 1 takes roughly 27% of clicks. Position 8 takes about 1%. So of those 480 people, they're getting around 130 and you're getting maybe 5.
>
> You told me a new [customer] is worth [$800]. Even if only one in ten of those turned into a job — that's a gap of about **$10,000 a month** going to them instead of you.
>
> And the second thing: they've got 87 Google reviews, you've got 11. That's a big part of why the ranking looks the way it does, and it's also what people compare when they're choosing.
>
> Does that match what you're seeing on your side?"

**Why this works:** you're not selling SEO. You're showing them a number — *their* number — that's currently leaving the building every month. Your fee stops being a cost and becomes a fraction of a loss they can now see.

**Then, the transition question:**

> "So — do you want to fix that, or is now not the time?"

Ask it and stop talking.

---

### Phase 4 — Prescription (5 min)

Do NOT list services. Prescribe a fix to the specific problem you just established.

> "Here's what I'd actually do, in order.
>
> **First 30 days** — fix the foundation. Your site's missing [specific thing you found], and your Google Business Profile isn't set up to compete. That's the fastest movement available. I'd also get a review generation system in place, because that 11-vs-87 gap is the biggest single lever you have.
>
> **Days 30–90** — go after '[keyword]' and the 4 or 5 terms around it. That means [location pages / service pages / technical fixes — whatever's actually true for them].
>
> **Days 90–180** — that's when the ranking movement compounds and the calls start showing up consistently. I want to be honest with you: **this is not a 30-day thing.** Anyone who tells you they'll get you to page one in 60 days is either lying or about to get your site penalised. Six months is when this looks like a real result.
>
> You'd get a monthly call with me where I show you what moved, and a weekly video update so you're never wondering what's happening."

**Note what the last line does:** it turns your one genuine strength — personal communication — into an explicitly sold feature rather than a free bonus you give away. That's not spin. It's the thing your clients already say is why they stay. **Charge for it.**

---

### Phase 5 — Price (2 min) — the part you're most afraid of

**Rules:**
1. Say the number **plainly**. No apologising, no "it's normally X but for you...", no rambling.
2. Present **three options**, not one. Anchor high.
3. **Then stop talking.** Completely. Whoever speaks first loses. The silence will feel like an hour. Let it.

> "There are three ways we could do this.
>
> **Growth** is $3,000 a month — that's SEO plus paid ads together, which is the fastest path because ads bring leads while the SEO builds.
>
> **Standard** is $2,500 a month — SEO only, full scope, everything I just described.
>
> **Local** is $1,200 a month — narrower, focused on [City] and your top three services only. Slower, but it's the entry point.
>
> Six-month minimum on all three, because anything shorter and I can't get you a result I'd be willing to put my name on.
>
> Which of those feels closest?"

Then: **silence.** Count to ten in your head if you have to.

**Practise saying "$2,500 a month" out loud, 20 times, before your next call.** Genuinely. The reason cheap agencies stay cheap is that the founder's voice wobbles on the number. Yours can't.

### Phase 6 — The six objections you will actually hear

#### "It's too expensive"
> "I get that. Can I ask — too expensive compared to what? Another quote, or just more than you'd budgeted?"
>
> *[If another quote:]* "Who else are you looking at? … Right. What I'd say is — [X] is cheaper, and at that price they're doing [reality of what $500 buys]. That might be exactly right for you. But you told me a customer is worth $800. At $1,200 a month, this pays for itself at **two extra jobs**. If I can't get you two extra jobs a month, you should fire me."
>
> *[If budget:]* "Then let's do the Local package at $1,200 and expand when it's working. I'd rather start smaller and earn the rest."

**Never drop the price. Drop the scope.**

#### "I need to think about it"
> "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through? — I ask because usually it's one of three things: you're not sure it'll work, you're not sure about the money, or you're not sure about me. Whichever it is, I'd rather answer it now than have you sit on it."

Nine times out of ten they tell you the real objection, and it's one you can answer.

#### "I've been burned before / the last agency did nothing"
> "Tell me what happened." *[Listen fully. Don't defend the industry.]*
>
> "Yeah, that's common and it's why people don't trust this business. Two things I'd do differently: you'll have a monthly call with me where I show you actual numbers, not a PDF of vanity metrics. And you can cancel after six months with 30 days' notice — I'm not going to trap you in a 12-month contract to protect myself."

#### "Can you guarantee results / page 1?"
> "No, and anyone who does is lying to you — nobody controls Google's algorithm. What I'll guarantee is the work: [specific deliverables] every month, and a call where I show you exactly what moved and what didn't. If in 90 days the leading indicators aren't moving, I'll tell you before you have to ask."

**Refusing to guarantee rankings makes you more credible, not less.** It's the single fastest way to separate yourself from the agencies that burned them.

#### "Send me a proposal"
Usually a soft no. Convert it:
> "Happy to. So it's actually useful rather than generic — can we book 15 minutes for me to walk you through it? I'll send it beforehand. Otherwise it just sits in an inbox and we both waste the effort."

**Never send a proposal without a booked call to review it.** You have a proposal generator that makes genuinely strong branded documents — use it, but never let it be the last touch.

#### "Why should I use you over a US agency?"
Answer it head-on. Don't get defensive.
> "Two honest reasons. One, price — you're getting senior-level attention for what a US agency charges for a junior account manager. Two, you deal with **me**, the owner, every single week — not an account coordinator who's juggling 30 accounts. The trade-off is time zone, and I work US hours for calls. If that's a dealbreaker, that's fair, and I'd tell you now."

---

### Phase 7 — Close (2 min)

**Always close to a specific next step. Never end with "let me know."**

**If yes:**
> "Great. Here's what happens next: I'll send the agreement and the first invoice today — you'll have it within the hour. Once that's signed we'll do a 45-minute kickoff where I get access to your accounts and we set the 90-day targets. I've got Thursday 2pm or Friday 10am. Which works?"

**Book the kickoff on the call.** Not later.

**If "think about it":**
> "Totally fine. Let's do this — I'll send a short summary of what we discussed today. And rather than leaving it open, let's put 15 minutes in the diary for Tuesday. If it's a no by then, tell me on that call and I'll stop bothering you — genuinely, no hard feelings. Does Tuesday 3pm work?"

**Always leave with a date on the calendar.** A "no" with a date beats a "maybe" without one.

**If no:**
> "Appreciate you being straight with me. Can I ask what tipped it? — it helps me get better at this." *[Then:]* "If anything changes, you know where I am. And if you hear of anyone in [industry] struggling with the same thing, I'd appreciate the introduction."

## After the call — within 1 hour

Send this. Every time. Same day.

> **Subject:** [Company] — recap + next step
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Good talking. Quick recap so we're on the same page:
>
> **What we found:**
> - Position 8 for "[keyword]" — [Competitor] is at 1
> - ~480 searches/month locally
> - 11 Google reviews vs their 87
> - At [$800] per customer, roughly $10K/month going elsewhere
>
> **What I'd do:**
> - Days 0–30: [x]
> - Days 30–90: [y]
> - Days 90–180: [z]
>
> **Investment:** $2,500/mo, 6-month minimum.
>
> **Next step:** [booked call Tuesday 3pm / agreement attached]
>
> Anything I got wrong above, tell me.
>
> Farhan

## Follow-up sequence for non-closers

**80% of deals close after the 5th touch. Almost nobody follows up more than twice. This is free money you are currently leaving on the table.**

| Day | Touch |
|---|---|
| 1 | Recap email (above) |
| 3 | The proposal (use your proposal generator) |
| 5 | Short video walking through the proposal |
| 8 | One relevant case study, 3 sentences |
| 14 | "Saw [competitor] just launched [thing] — thought of you." Value, not a pitch |
| 21 | "Still worth doing?" |
| 30 | Breakup: "I'll stop reaching out — if it comes back around, I'm here." |
| 60 | New angle: something specific that changed on their site or in their rankings |

## Practice plan — do this before your next call

1. **Read Phase 1 out loud 10 times** until you don't need to read it.
2. **Say "$2,500 a month" out loud 20 times** until it sounds boring coming out of your mouth.
3. **Record yourself** doing a mock call. Watch it. You'll hate it. Do it anyway — it's the fastest improvement available.
4. **Memorise the 13 discovery questions.** Not the answers — you don't need answers, you need questions.
5. **Run the first 3 calls at your old prices if you must**, purely as reps. Then never again.

---

## On camera — since you asked

- Camera at eye level. Stack of books under the laptop. Looking down at people reads as low status.
- Light in front of you, not behind. A window works.
- Plain background. Nothing distracting.
- Notes on a second screen or taped beside the camera. Nobody can tell.
- Smile once at the start and once at the end. That's enough.
- **Slow down.** Nervous people speak fast. Speaking slowly reads as confidence, and it buys you thinking time.
- Silence after you say the price. It's the whole game.
