# 02 — The Money Math: How $10,000 Actually Happens

---

## First, the honest arithmetic

**Current:** ~$1,000/mo. **Target:** $10,000/mo. **Gap:** $9,000.

### Path A — do nothing different (current pricing)

$9,000 ÷ $500 avg = **18 new retainer clients in 30 days.**

Working backwards through realistic funnel rates:

| Stage | Rate | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| New clients | — | 18 |
| Close rate from meeting (with a real script) | 20–25% | **~80 meetings** |
| Meeting rate from cold email | 1.0–2.5% of sends | **3,500–8,000 emails** |
| Emails needed at 80% inbox placement | — | **4,400–10,000 sends** |
| Your current monthly volume | — | ~600 total, ever |

And then you'd need to onboard 18 clients in one month, alone, with outsourced delivery you already describe as mediocre. You would lose most of them by month three.

**Verdict: not possible, and not desirable even if it were.**

### Path B — reprice first

$9,000 ÷ $1,800 avg = **5 new clients.**

| Stage | Rate | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| New clients | — | 5 |
| Close rate from meeting | 20–25% | **20–25 meetings** |
| Meeting rate from outreach | 1.0–2.5% | **1,000–2,500 emails** |

Same revenue. **One quarter of the sales work.** Five clients you can personally service well, which means they stay, which means month two starts at $10,000 instead of starting over.

**This is the plan.** Everything else in this document supports it.

### Path C — the fastest cash (30–45 days)

Recurring revenue takes time to stack. *Cash collected* doesn't. Here's a realistic mix that puts **$10,000 in the bank inside 45 days** — 7 sales, not 20:

| Source | Detail | Cash | Recurring? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Win-back** | 2 past clients returned at new pricing ($900 avg) | $1,800 | ✅ $1,800/mo |
| **Price increase** | Existing clients moved up after 60-day notice | $500 | ✅ $500/mo |
| **Websites** | 2 sites @ $2,500 one-time | $5,000 | ❌ one-time |
| **White-label partner** | 1 agency partner, 3 clients @ $700 wholesale | $2,100 | ✅ $2,100/mo |
| **New retainer** | 1 direct client from outreach @ $1,500 | $1,500 | ✅ $1,500/mo |
| **Total collected** | **7 sales** | **$10,900** | **$5,900/mo recurring** |

**Be clear-eyed about this:** that's $10,900 in *cash*, but only $5,900 of it repeats next month. That's still a 6x improvement on where you are and it's genuinely achievable. Month two you replace the $5,000 of website cash with 3 more retainers and you're at $10,000 recurring. That's the 90-day plan.

**Why websites carry the cash plan:** one-time projects close faster than retainers (lower commitment, clearer deliverable, obvious finish line), they're the easiest thing to sell to a business that isn't ready for SEO, and they create the relationship that sells the retainer in month two. Your custom-priced website service is currently your most under-used asset.

## The new price list

Effective immediately for all new prospects. Existing clients: 60-day notice, then move.

| Service | Old | **New** | Market context |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO — Local (1 location, ≤3 service areas) | $500 | **$1,200/mo** | Low end of the $1,500–5,000 working range; defensible for narrow local scope |
| SEO — Growth (multi-location or competitive) | $500 | **$2,500/mo** | Below the ~$3,000 market average — still the "value" option |
| Google/Meta Ads management | $300 | **$1,000/mo** or 15% of spend, whichever is higher | Min. $2,000/mo ad spend required |
| Email marketing / lifecycle | $300 | **$800/mo** | Setup fee $1,000 first month |
| Website — Standard (5–8 pages) | custom | **$2,500** one-time | 50% deposit up front |
| Website — Premium (10+ pages, custom design) | custom | **$4,000–6,000** | 50% deposit up front |
| **Growth Package** (SEO + Ads + reporting) | — | **$3,000/mo** | Your anchor offer. Sell this first. |
| Website + 6-month SEO | — | **$1,500 setup + $1,500/mo** | Best cash-flow structure you can sell |

### Rules that matter more than the numbers

1. **Always present three options.** Never one price. Anchor high, sell the middle. (Your proposal generator skill already builds 3-tier pricing cards — use them, don't discount off them.)
2. **6-month minimum on SEO.** Non-negotiable. SEO can't produce results in 3 months, and short contracts guarantee churn and guarantee you get blamed for it. If they won't sign 6 months, they're an ads client or not a client.
3. **50% deposit on all one-time work.** Always. No exceptions.
4. **Never discount the price. Reduce the scope.** "I can do $1,200 instead of $2,500 — that's the Local package, which covers 3 service areas instead of 10." Discounting teaches the client your price is fiction and destroys the next negotiation.
5. **Ads: require a minimum ad spend.** A client spending $500/mo on ads will never see a result and will churn angry, blaming you. Turn them away or sell them a website instead.

### What to do about existing clients

Don't blow up your only $1,000. Sequence it:

- **Today:** all *new* prospects get new pricing. No exceptions, no "just this one."
- **Day 30:** email existing clients — "Starting [date 60 days out], our SEO retainer moves to $1,200/mo, reflecting expanded scope (list 3 genuine additions: monthly reporting call, GBP management, content calendar). You're locked at your current rate until then."
- **Day 90:** they move up or they leave. If they leave at $500, you lost $500 and freed capacity worth $1,200. **That is a good trade and you should want it.**

The fear here is real but the math isn't scary: you have ~2 clients. Worst case you lose both and you're at $0 with a full pipeline. You're at $1,000 now, which is functionally the same as $0. **You have almost nothing to lose, which is exactly why now is the right time to reprice.** It only gets harder when you have 10 clients.

## Justifying the price to yourself (you'll need this)

The number one reason agency owners fail to raise prices is that they don't believe the price. Here's the reframe:

**A US personal injury firm's average case value is $10,000–30,000+. A roofing company's average job is $8,000–15,000. A med spa client is worth $2,000–5,000 lifetime.**

If your SEO produces **one extra customer per month**, you have paid for yourself 3–10x over at $2,500/mo. That's the entire argument. You are not selling "SEO for $2,500." You are selling "one more roof job a month for $2,500."

At $500/mo you're not even a rounding error in their P&L — which is precisely why they don't take you seriously, don't give you assets on time, and churn without a second thought. **Cheap clients are not more loyal. They're less loyal.**

## Unit economics you must know before spending a dollar

| Metric | How to compute | Your target |
|---|---|---|
| **ACV** (annual contract value) | monthly fee × 12 | $14,400 at $1,200/mo |
| **Gross margin** | (fee − contractor cost) ÷ fee | **≥ 60%.** At $500 fee / $300 contractor you're at 40% — unviable |
| **CAC** (cost to acquire a client) | total sales+marketing spend ÷ clients won | Keep under 1 month of revenue |
| **LTV** | monthly fee × avg months retained | 12+ months |
| **LTV:CAC** | LTV ÷ CAC | **3:1 minimum** |
| **Payback period** | CAC ÷ monthly gross profit | Under 3 months |

**Run this on yourself right now:** if your average client stays 4 months at $500 with a $300 contractor cost, LTV gross profit = $800. If you spend $200 acquiring them, that's 4:1 — fine on paper. But $800 of gross profit doesn't pay you a salary at any volume you can personally service. **The problem was never CAC. It was price and retention.**

At $1,500/mo × 12 months × 60% margin = **$10,800 gross profit per client.** Now you can afford to spend real money on acquisition, hire a better contractor, and actually deliver. Everything gets easier above the line and nothing works below it.
