## 0. Reactivation — free, fastest. **DO THIS TODAY.**

You had $5,000/mo of clients. Every one of them already trusted you enough to send you money. That is the most qualified list you will ever own and it costs nothing to contact.

**Build three lists right now (2 hours):**
- **List A:** every past client, last 24 months
- **List B:** every prospect who ever replied, took a call, or received a quote and didn't sign
- **List C:** every current client (for referrals + upsell)

### List A script — past clients

> **Subject:** quick question, [First Name]
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> It's Farhan from Mavix. I've been going back through the accounts we worked on and wanted to reach out directly.
>
> Two things:
>
> 1. Genuinely — what could we have done better? I'm rebuilding how we run accounts this quarter and honest feedback from people who've actually worked with us is worth more than anything else I could do. Even one blunt sentence helps.
>
> 2. We've narrowed our focus to [niche] and restructured how we deliver — [name one concrete change: dedicated account lead, weekly video updates, a proper reporting call]. If [Company] is still looking at organic growth, I'd like to show you what that looks like now. No pitch on the call — I'll walk you through what I'd do differently and you can decide.
>
> Either way, thanks for working with us.
>
> Farhan

**Why this works:** the feedback ask is genuine and it's disarming — it gets replies from people who'd ignore a pitch. It also gets you the churn data you desperately need. And "we've changed" is the only credible reason to return to a vendor who disappointed you.

**Send it individually, from your real inbox, one at a time.** Not a sequencer. Twenty of these by hand today.

### List B script — dead leads

> **Subject:** re: [what you discussed], [Month Year]
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> We spoke back in [month] about [specific thing — their rankings, their ads, their site]. Timing wasn't right then.
>
> Quick reason I'm reaching back out: we now work almost exclusively with [niche] companies, and [specific relevant result or observation]. I pulled up [their domain] again this morning and [one specific, true, checkable observation — e.g. "you're still not ranking for '[keyword]' and there are 320 searches/month for it in [city]"].
>
> Worth 15 minutes?
>
> Farhan

**Expected from Tier 0 reactivation: 1–3 clients within 10 days.** This alone can double your current revenue.

## 1. Cold Email — the rebuild

Your setup isn't wrong in kind. It's wrong in configuration and it's massively under-used.

### The deliverability audit (do this BEFORE sending anything)

Under the 2026 enforced rules at Google/Yahoo/Microsoft, failing mail is **rejected outright**, not spam-foldered. Check every one of these on all 15 mailboxes:

- [ ] **SPF** record exists, passes, and is aligned
- [ ] **DKIM** signing enabled and passing
- [ ] **DMARC** set to **`p=quarantine` or `p=reject`** — `p=none` is no longer sufficient in 2026
- [ ] **One-click List-Unsubscribe (RFC 8058)** header present — Microsoft treats its absence as a cold-email red flag
- [ ] Custom tracking domain (or **tracking off entirely** — open tracking hurts placement more than it helps, and Apple Mail Privacy Protection has made open rates meaningless anyway)
- [ ] Sending domains are **variants**, never your primary `mavixmarketing.com` — never risk your main domain
- [ ] Each mailbox warmed 4–6 weeks: start 5–10/day, ramp gradually, keep volume *predictable*
- [ ] Every list verified before send — **bounces must stay under 2%**
- [ ] Spam complaints monitored — must stay **under 0.3%**
- [ ] Seed-test inbox placement — target **80%+**

**If you skip this and just increase volume, you will burn 15 mailboxes and conclude a second time that cold email doesn't work.**

### Volume targets

| Metric | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Mailboxes | 15 | 15 (enough — don't buy more yet) |
| Emails/mailbox/day | ~2 | 20–30 |
| **Emails/day** | ~40 | **300–450** |
| **Emails/month** | ~600 total | **6,600–9,000** |

At a 3.5% reply rate: ~250 replies/mo → ~60 positive → **15–25 meetings/mo.**

**You do not need more infrastructure. You need to press send.**

### List building (where to get emails — you asked directly)

| Source | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Instantly Lead Finder** | $37.90/mo (1k) → $82.50 (10k) | Best value at your budget. Integrated with sending |
| **Apollo** | Free = 1,200 credits/**year**; $49/mo Basic | Best filters. Note 1 credit = email, 6 = email+mobile |
| **Your own Google Maps scraper** | ~$50/mo in API costs | **Best data quality for local niches** — see §6 |
| **Google Maps + manual enrichment** | free/cheap | Highest quality, lowest volume |
| **Industry association directories** | free | Extremely high intent. Underused. State bar directories, contractor licensing boards, chamber of commerce lists |
| **Local BBB / Yelp / niche directories** | free | Same |

**Do not buy scraped bulk lists.** They bounce, they blow your 2% bounce ceiling, and one bad list can kill domains you spent 6 weeks warming.

### The email that actually works

Short. One idea. One specific, checkable observation. No pitch in email one.

> **Subject:** [Company] — [keyword] on Google
>
> Hi [First],
>
> Looked at [domain] this morning — you're on page 3 for "[keyword]" in [city]. [Competitor] is at the top and it's about [one specific technical reason: "they have 40 reviews on their GBP and you have 6" / "they've got location pages for each suburb and you don't"].
>
> There are about [N] searches a month for that term locally. At your average job value that's meaningful.
>
> Want me to send a 3-minute video breaking down exactly what they're doing that you're not? Free either way — I'll send it whether or not we ever work together.
>
> Farhan
> Mavix Marketing

**Why this works:**
- Specific and checkable → proves you actually looked, which almost nobody does
- The ask is for *permission to send value*, not for a meeting → far lower friction
- The free video is your actual product demo and it's what converts
- No price, no service list, no "we're a full service agency"

**Follow-up cadence — 2–3 follow-ups generate up to 42% of all replies.** Do not stop at one.

| Day | Message |
|---|---|
| 0 | The email above |
| 3 | "Made the video anyway — [Loom link]. No strings." ← **highest-converting message in the sequence** |
| 7 | One relevant case study, 3 sentences |
| 12 | "Should I close the file on this?" ← breakup emails get surprising reply rates |

**Sending the video unprompted on day 3 is the single highest-leverage change you can make to your cold email.** It costs you 10 minutes per prospect and it converts because nobody else does it. Only do it for prospects worth $1,500/mo — which is another reason the repricing matters.

### Handling replies — DO NOT AUTOMATE THIS

You mentioned building auto-reply automation. Don't. Positive replies are roughly 1 in 100–200 emails sent — the rarest asset in your funnel. Automating them is optimising the exact step where being human wins.

**Reply by hand, within 5 minutes if possible.** Speed-to-lead is the biggest close-rate variable in this industry.

Response template for "sure, send it":

> Sending it over in the next hour. One quick thing so I make it useful — what's your average value per new [customer/case/job]? Changes which keywords I'd focus on.

That question does three jobs: it makes the video better, it qualifies budget, and it starts the ROI conversation before you ever name a price.

## 2. Referrals & Partner Network — free, highest ROI ongoing

Everyone knows this and almost nobody does it systematically.

### Direct ask (current + past clients)

Never ask "do you know anyone?" — the answer is always no. Ask specifically:

> "I'm taking on two more [niche] clients this quarter. Who's one other [roofer/attorney/clinic owner] you know who's decent at what they do but invisible on Google? I'll take care of them the same way I take care of you — and I'll knock $200/mo off your retainer for 3 months if they sign."

Specific ask + named incentive. Do this by phone, not email.

### Partner network (the underrated one)

Find people who **already have your clients** but **don't sell what you sell**:

| Partner type | Why they need you |
|---|---|
| Freelance web designers | Clients constantly ask "can you do SEO too?" — they say no and lose the revenue |
| Branding / PR agencies | Same, for performance marketing |
| Copywriters | Same, for the technical side |
| Business coaches / consultants (niche-specific) | Their clients need leads; that's the whole problem they're hired to solve |
| Accountants / bookkeepers serving your niche | Trusted advisor, constant "who should I use for marketing?" questions |
| CRM / software resellers in your niche | Sell to exactly your buyer |

**Offer:** 10–20% recurring referral commission for the life of the client, or a flat $300–500 finder's fee. Recurring is better — it makes them keep sending.

**Where to find them:** Upwork (search "web designer", look at agency profiles), LinkedIn, Facebook groups for freelancers, local business networks. Twenty messages a week.

> Hi [Name] — you build sites for [niche], I do SEO/ads for [niche], and we don't compete. When your clients ask "can you also handle Google?" — I'd like to be your answer. 15% recurring on anything that closes, white-labelled under your brand if you prefer. Worth a chat?

## 3. White-Label — the highest-leverage channel you are ignoring

**Read this section twice.** This is the biggest opportunity in your situation and I don't think you've considered it.

### Why it fits you specifically

You already outsource all delivery. **You are structurally already a white-label shop** — you just happen to be selling retail. Adding wholesale requires zero new capability.

### The economics

| | Retail (what you do now) | White-label (what you should add) |
|---|---|---|
| Sales cycle | 2–6 weeks, one client at a time | 2–4 weeks, then **repeat orders forever** |
| Deal size | $500/mo | $700–1,500/mo × **multiple clients per partner** |
| Client comms | **You**, constantly | **The partner does it** |
| Churn | High (you own the relationship) | Lower (partner owns it) |
| Sales required per $3,000/mo | 6 clients | **1 partner** |

The market rates: agencies buying white-label SEO typically pay **$300–700/mo** for entry tier and resell at **$800–1,500**, or pay **$700–1,500** and resell at **$1,500–3,000**. Standard markup is 100–200%, targeting 40–60% gross margin. Agencies using white-label partners grew SEO revenue ~32% on average.

**You sit on the supply side of that trade.** Charge $700–900/mo wholesale. A partner with 4 clients = **$2,800–3,600/mo from one relationship** — and crucially, **they handle the client communication**, which is exactly the thing that caps you at 5–8 clients.

### The catch, stated plainly

White-label buyers are agencies. They will scrutinise your work harder than any retail client, because their reputation rides on it. **Your current delivery quality will get you fired from a white-label deal fast.** So: start with **one** partner, over-deliver massively on their first 2 clients, then scale. Don't pitch 20 partners and win 5 — you'd blow up all five.

### Where to find partners

1. **Upwork** — agencies actively post looking for white-label SEO partners. Search "white label SEO", "SEO partner", "agency needs SEO fulfilment"
2. **LinkedIn** — target job titles "Agency Owner", "Founder" at 2–20 person agencies. Web design and branding agencies specifically
3. **Cold email to small agencies** — best-fit target: web design shops with 2–15 employees that offer sites but not SEO. Easy to identify from their service page
4. **Facebook/Slack agency owner communities**
5. **Directories** — Clutch, DesignRush. Filter agencies by "Web Design" only, no SEO listed

### The white-label pitch email

> **Subject:** SEO fulfilment for [Agency Name]'s clients
>
> Hi [First],
>
> You build sites for [industry]. I'm guessing some percentage of those clients ask "can you handle our Google rankings too?" — and right now that revenue either walks or gets subcontracted somewhere unreliable.
>
> I run SEO fulfilment for agencies. White-labelled, your branding on every report, I never touch your client directly. $[750]/mo per client, you resell at whatever you want — most partners are at $1,500–2,000.
>
> I'll do the first client at half rate for 60 days so you can judge the work rather than my pitch.
>
> Worth 15 minutes?
>
> Farhan — Mavix Marketing

That half-rate first client is a real offer and you should honour it. It removes all their risk, and one great result buys you a partner who sends you clients for years.

**Realistic target: 1–2 partners in 30 days = $2,000–4,000/mo recurring from 1–2 sales.**

## 4. Upwork — fix the zero-review problem in 30 days

You asked directly about buying an account. Covered in [`README.md`](README.md#on-the-gray-hat-question) — short version: bad trade, and there's a fast legitimate route.

### The zero-review problem is not a review problem

It's a **targeting and positioning** problem. Here's the 30-day fix:

**Week 1–2: farm reviews with small fixed-scope work.** Clients take a chance on unreviewed freelancers when the risk is small. Bid **$100–500 fixed-price** jobs only:
- "SEO audit for my site" — $150
- "Fix my Google Business Profile" — $200
- "Set up Google Analytics / Search Console" — $100
- "Keyword research for [industry]" — $250

Deliver these *extremely* well and fast. You're not buying revenue, **you're buying 5-star reviews at $150 each.** That's a marketing cost and it's cheaper than any ad you could run.

**Week 3–4: with 4–5 reviews, bid retainer work.** $1,000–2,500/mo SEO contracts.

**Also: pitch white-label partners on Upwork simultaneously** (see §3). Agencies posting for SEO help are on there constantly.

### Connects strategy

Connects cost **$0.15 each**. At a 1-in-20 win rate you're paying ~$48 in connects per client won — trivially profitable against a $1,500/mo contract.

**But:** a 30% win rate on 10 targeted proposals beats a 3% win rate on 100 generic ones. Spend on **5–10 highly-targeted proposals per week**, not mass-bidding.

**Only bid on jobs where:**
- Client payment is **verified**
- They've **hired before** with good history
- Posted **under 6 hours ago** (early bids get read)
- Under 15 proposals so far
- The job matches your niche

### Proposal structure (first 2 lines are everything — that's all they see in preview)

> **Line 1:** [Specific observation about their business/site — proves you read it and looked]
>
> **Line 2:** [The result you'd target and roughly how]
>
> Then: 3 bullets on approach. One relevant proof point. One question at the end (questions get replies).
>
> No "I am a passionate digital marketer with 5 years experience."

**Also list on Fiverr** — same review-farming logic, productised gigs ("Local SEO audit — $95"). Lower ceiling, but it's free to list and it's another review-generating surface.

### Two Upwork rules that will actually get you banned

- **Never take payment off-platform** for work sourced there. It's the most aggressively enforced policy, and there's a **24-month non-circumvention period**. This is the #1 fastest ban.
- **One user, one account.** No second accounts, no bought accounts.

## 5. LinkedIn — fix it, don't abandon it

Full diagnosis in [`01-diagnosis.md`](01-diagnosis.md#problem-6-the-linkedin-plan-has-a-specific-fixable-defect). Two fixes:

**Fix 1: you're likely on the wrong plan.** LinkedIn **Premium Career** is the job-seeker product — no Sales Navigator search or filters. Get **Sales Navigator Core** (~$100/mo) when you're at Tier 2, or work with free search until then. Don't pay for Career.

**Fix 2: the tactic you're using died. The channel didn't.**

| Metric | May 2025 | Apr 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Connection-**note** reply | 3.5% | **2.2%** (−37%) |
| Post-connection **message** reply | 10.4% | **10.9%** (flat) |
| Multi-action (profile view + DM) | — | **up to 11.87%** |

### The re-sequenced cadence (reconfigure your existing tool)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | View their profile. Nothing else |
| 1 | Like or comment on one recent post — genuinely, one sentence |
| 2 | Connection request, **no note** (or a note with zero pitch: "Been seeing your posts on [topic] — connecting.") |
| +2 after accept | Message. **Still no pitch.** One specific observation or question about their business |
| +4 | Value: "made you this" — short Loom or a 2-line insight |
| +8 | Soft ask for a call |

**Never pitch in the connection request.** That's the tactic that broke.

You said you can run 3 accounts. **Prove the sequence on one first.** Three accounts running a broken sequence just burns three accounts.

## 6. Google Maps / Local Outreach — your build is the right one

Of everything you're building, **this is the keeper.** Here's why:

- Local businesses are the correct buyer for a $1,200–2,500/mo local SEO retainer
- The data is **observable** — you can *see* their GBP review count, their rating, their site, their photos, whether they rank. That gives you specific, checkable personalisation, which is exactly what makes cold email work
- Nobody can buy this data cleanly, so you own an asset instead of renting a list

### What to add to your scraper so it produces *closable* leads, not just emails

Scrape or derive these fields per business:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| GBP rating + review count | The #1 hook. "You have 11 reviews, the top 3 in your city average 87" |
| Website present? Mobile-friendly? Page speed? | Instant website-sale trigger |
| Ranking position for 1 core local keyword | The strongest possible hook |
| Number of GBP photos / last post date | Neglect signal = they need help |
| Whether they run Google Ads | Already spending = has budget |
| Owner name (from GBP or site About page) | Personalisation |

**The highest-converting local email uses two of those fields:**

> Subject: [Business Name] — 11 reviews vs [Competitor]'s 87
>
> Hi [Owner],
>
> You're at 11 Google reviews. The three [trade]s ranking above you in [City] average 87. That's most of the gap — Google weights it heavily and so do customers.
>
> There are ~[N] people searching "[keyword]" in [City] every month. You're currently [position].
>
> Want the 3-minute breakdown video? Free regardless.
>
> Farhan

**One warning on the automation:** verify every email before sending. Scraped `info@` addresses bounce hard, and bounces above 2% will damage domains you spent 6 weeks warming. Route every scrape through verification. Non-negotiable.

**Also: `info@` addresses convert far worse than named addresses.** Where you can find the owner's name, guess-and-verify the pattern (`firstname@domain`) rather than defaulting to the generic inbox.

## 7. Instagram — lowest priority

Honest assessment: for B2B agency services to US local businesses, Instagram DM is the **lowest-intent, worst-converting** channel on this list, and you said the API costs real money.

**Do not spend money here yet.** If you want to run it, run it free and manually at 10–15 DMs/day to local businesses in your niche — same rules: specific observation, no pitch, offer value.

**Where Instagram *is* worth it:** if your niche is visual and consumer-facing (med spas, salons, restaurants, dental, fitness). Then the DM works because you can reference their actual content. If your niche is legal/HVAC/B2B, skip it entirely.

**Defer this until you're at $6,000/mo.** It's a distraction right now.

## 8. Paid Ads — bottom-funnel only, Tier 3+

Don't run ads until you're at ~$6,000/mo. When you do:

**Only bid on high-intent search terms:**
- `[niche] seo agency` / `[niche] marketing agency`
- `seo agency [city]`
- `[competitor name]` (brand bidding on competitors is legal, cheap, and effective)
- `hire seo consultant [niche]`

**Never** run broad awareness, display, or "brand" campaigns at $300/mo. You'll spend the money and learn nothing.

**Landing page:** one page, one offer, one form. Your offer should be the free audit/video — the same thing that works in cold email. Form fields: name, email, website, phone. Nothing else. Every extra field costs you conversions.

You mentioned you have a strategy and plan for this already — good. Just hold it until the sales process is fixed, otherwise you'll pay for clicks and fumble the calls.

## 9. Organic / Content — you're right to skip it

You said you can't do organic and don't have time. **Correct call, for now.** SEO for your own agency takes 6–12 months and you need money in 30 days.

**One exception worth 2 hours/week:** post 3x/week on LinkedIn about specific results and specific tactics in your niche. Not for reach — **for credibility.** Every cold prospect will look at your profile before replying, and a profile with recent, specific, competent posts converts far better than an empty one. That's not content marketing, that's **sales collateral**, and it's worth the two hours.

Revisit real content marketing at $15,000/mo.
