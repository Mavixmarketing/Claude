# 06 — Onboarding & Retention: Why You Lost $4,000/mo

This is the file that matters most for whether you're still in business in a year.

---

## The math nobody wants to look at

Say you close 3 new clients a month at $1,500. Feels great.

| If average retention is... | Your ceiling is |
|---|---|
| 3 months | $13,500 — and you have to close 3 *every* month forever just to stay flat |
| 6 months | $27,000 |
| 12 months | $54,000 |
| 18 months | $81,000 |

**Same sales effort. 6x difference in outcome.** Retention isn't a "nice to have" that comes after growth — retention *is* growth. A client who stays 12 months instead of 4 is worth exactly as much as acquiring two more clients, and costs nothing to get.

You went $5,000 → $1,000. Whatever the specific reasons, the structural fact is: **you were acquiring, but you weren't keeping.** Fix that and the same sales effort that got you to $5k gets you past $10k and holds.

## Why clients actually leave agencies

In order of frequency. Note that only one of these is about results:

| # | Reason | Share | Your exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **They don't know what you're doing.** Silence reads as "nothing is happening" | ~40% | Medium — you communicate well, but ad hoc |
| 2 | **No visible result and no explanation of why** | ~25% | **HIGH** — you said quality is "not that good" |
| 3 | **Bad onboarding.** The first 30 days set the entire tone | ~15% | **HIGH** — you said this is a problem |
| 4 | Their business changed / budget cut | ~10% | Uncontrollable |
| 5 | Relationship — they don't feel valued | ~10% | Low — this is your strength |

**Two of the top three are communication problems, not delivery problems.** That's genuinely good news for you, because communication is the thing you're already good at. You just do it reactively and inconsistently. Systematise it and you fix most of your churn without touching delivery quality.

## Your specific situation

Your words:

> *"I try to manage with communication by myself. That is the only reason they are here with me."*

Read that again. **You have accidentally identified your product.** Your clients are not buying SEO from you — they're buying *being taken care of*. That is a completely legitimate thing to sell. Most agencies are terrible at it.

The problem is you're doing it **reactively and manually**, which means:
- it's inconsistent — good weeks and bad weeks
- it doesn't scale past ~5–8 clients
- it isn't priced in, so you give away your most valuable asset for free
- when you get busy chasing new clients, existing clients get less of it — **which is exactly the mechanism that turned $5k into $1k**

**The fix is not to communicate less. It's to make the communication a fixed, scheduled system that happens whether or not you're busy.**

## The 30-day onboarding system

The first 30 days determine whether they stay 4 months or 24. Most agencies do nothing for 3 weeks and then send a report. Here's what to do instead.

### Day 0 — the hour after they sign

Momentum is at its absolute peak the moment they pay. Never waste it.

- [ ] Signed agreement + invoice sent within **1 hour**
- [ ] Welcome email with the kickoff call already booked
- [ ] A single access checklist (GBP, GA4, Search Console, website admin, ads account) — **one link, not five emails**
- [ ] A "what happens next" 1-pager: exactly what occurs in weeks 1, 2, 3, 4

### Day 1–2 — kickoff call (45 min)

- Goals and what success means to *them* in their words
- Their average customer value (confirm the number from the sales call)
- What they've tried and what they hated about previous agencies
- Who does what, and how you'll communicate
- **Set expectations hard:** "Months 1–3 you'll see leading indicators, not leads. Month 4–6 is when it converts. If I promised you leads in month 2, I'd be lying."

**Under-promising here prevents almost all month-3 churn.** The single most common cause of cancellation is a client who expected results by month 2 because nobody told them otherwise.

### Week 1 — the quick win

**Ship something visible in the first 7 days.** Always. Non-negotiable.

Options depending on service: GBP fully optimised, a technical fix with a before/after screenshot, page speed improvement, the review-request system turned on, a competitor gap analysis document.

**Why:** the client's biggest fear the week after paying is "did I just waste my money?" A visible win in week 1 kills that fear permanently, and a client who is not anxious does not churn.

### Weeks 1–4 — the rhythm (this is the whole system)

| Cadence | What | Time cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** | 2–3 minute Loom video: what I did this week, what I'm doing next week, anything I need from you | **10 min/client/week** |
| **Monthly** | Live report call — 30 min, screen share, actual numbers | 30 min/client/month |
| **Always** | Reply to any client message within 4 business hours | — |

**The weekly Loom is the highest-ROI 10 minutes in your entire business.** It is worth more to retention than any deliverable you produce, because it directly solves reason #1 (they don't know what you're doing). Ten clients = 100 minutes a week. That's it. That's the whole retention system.

**Never skip a week.** The week you skip is the week they start wondering what they're paying for.

## The monthly report call

Never just email a PDF. A PDF gets skimmed and generates doubt. A call generates trust and gives you upsell openings.

**Structure (30 min):**
1. **What moved** (5 min) — 3 metrics, no more. Rankings, traffic, calls/leads
2. **What I did** (5 min) — plain language, no jargon
3. **What didn't work** (5 min) — ← **say this out loud every month**
4. **Next month's plan** (10 min)
5. **What I need from you** (5 min)

**Point 3 is the trust unlock.** Volunteering what failed, before they notice, makes everything else you say credible. Agencies that only report good news get fired the moment a client checks anything independently.

## Fixing the delivery quality problem

You said quality is "just fine, not that good" and filed it under later. It can't be later, but it also doesn't need to be expensive — the fix comes free with the repricing.

**At $500/client with a $300 contractor, you have $200 to work with. At $1,500 with a $600 contractor, you have $900.** You can now afford someone competent. **The quality problem was a pricing problem the whole time.**

Three things to put in place:

**1. One niche = one playbook.** Right now every client gets a bespoke approach, which means your contractors start from scratch each time and quality is random. One niche means one documented process that gets better every month. This is the biggest quality lever available and it costs nothing but the decision to specialise.

**2. A QA checklist you personally run before anything reaches a client.** 15 minutes per deliverable. You are the last line of defence between mediocre contractor work and a client's inbox. Right now nothing sits between those two things.

**3. Pay one contractor more instead of several less.** One good person at $600–800/mo who learns your niche beats three cheap people who don't. Consistency is what clients experience as quality.

## The churn early-warning system

Watch for these. Any two together means intervene **this week**, not next month:

- 🚩 Slow to reply when they used to reply fast
- 🚩 Skipped or rescheduled the monthly call
- 🚩 "Can you send me a breakdown of what you've done so far?" ← **this is almost always a pre-cancellation audit**
- 🚩 A new person joins the calls (often a new marketing hire who wants their own vendor)
- 🚩 Asking about contract terms or notice periods
- 🚩 They've gone quiet for 3+ weeks

**Intervention:** call them. Not email. "Wanted to check in properly — how do you feel this is going? Be blunt with me." Most saveable churn is saved by one honest phone call made two weeks before the client had decided to leave.

## Do this first, before adding a single new client

1. **Call both your current clients this week.** Not email. Ask: "How do you honestly feel this is going? What would make it better?" Then fix whatever they say.
2. **Email every past client** — the win-back script in [`04-channels.md`](04-channels.md#0-reactivation-free-fastest-do-this-today) doubles as your exit interview. **Find out why they actually left.** You cannot fix a leak you haven't located.
3. **Start weekly Looms this Monday** for the clients you have.
4. **Write the onboarding checklist** so client #3 gets the same experience as client #1.

If you add 8 clients onto a system that loses them in 4 months, you'll be back at $1,000/mo by Christmas with more grey hair. Fix the retention while you sell. They're not sequential — but if you can only do one this week, **do this one.**
