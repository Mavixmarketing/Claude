# The back sections

Everything `script.md` names after minute 30 and does not spell out. Spoken lines are in quotes and are said as written. Everything else is a note to Farhan. Nothing here may contradict `facts.md`, break `mechanics.md`, or use a word on the `voice.md` list.

## 1. The four qualifying questions

End of discovery, after the deeper layer and before the yes/no/maybe frame. One at a time, three seconds of silence after each. He types, he does not react.

### One — money

> *"Before I show you anything, have you got a rough number in your head for this? I'd rather know now than build you something you were never going to spend on."*

**Tells him:** whether this call ends in a package or ends warmly. Search floor is the Starter price ($749, or a lower price already quoted to them), ads floor is $200 (see `facts.md`).

**Bad answer:** "I don't know, what does it cost?" is not actually a bad answer — most people have not thought about it. Say *"Fair enough. It's a monthly fee, no setup cost. For search it starts at seven-forty-nine a month (or the price they were already quoted); for ads it starts in the low hundreds. Does that sound like the right neighbourhood or the wrong one?"* and carry on. A number below the floor is the disqualify in section 5, never a discount.

### Two — who actually decides

> *"If you get to the end of this and you like it, is that you deciding on the spot, or is there someone else who'd need to be happy with it first?"*

**Tells him:** whether to say a price at all. `mechanics.md` lists pitching a price to someone who cannot say yes as a failure mode.

**Bad answer:** "I'd need to run it past my business partner." Run the whole call, say **no price**, book the next one with both before hanging up: *"Then let's not have you repeat this twice. I'll show you everything now, and we'll get [name] on a short one so they hear it from me instead of from you."*

### Three — timing

> *"If you did want to do this, when would you actually want it starting? This month, or is there a date you've got in your head?"*

**Tells him:** whether a yes today is a yes today or a yes in March. It also stops him closing hard on someone who has said plainly they are not starting until the new year.

**Bad answer:** "Sometime this year." Say *"Okay. What has to happen before it becomes this month rather than sometime?"* If nothing has to happen, it is a maybe, and a maybe gets a booked date rather than a chase.

### Four — what matters to them

> *"Last one. When you pick someone to do this for you, what actually matters to you? Not the sales answer — what would make you say yes to one agency and no to another?"*

**Tells him:** the whole close. This is the most valuable sentence on the call.

**Reused word for word at minute 21.** He opens the offer with their own sentence: *"You said the thing that matters to you is [their exact words]. So let me answer that first."* Their words, their order, not a paraphrase. If he cannot remember them he did not type them, and he should ask again rather than approximate.

**Bad answer:** "Just results, really." Push once, gently: *"Sure. But everyone says results. What's the thing that would annoy you enough to leave?"* That gets a real answer nearly every time.

## 2. Objections

Twelve, for search work and for any call. The six ads-only ones are in `ads-track.md` section 6 and are not repeated here. Shape is always **understand it, answer in two sentences, ask something back.** Never three sentences, and never answer without the question back — that is what stops it becoming a monologue.

### "That's more than I wanted to spend."

Isolate it exactly as `mechanics.md` says, because no discount ever fixes a belief problem. *"That's fair, and I'd rather you said it than sat there doing sums. Can I ask you something straight — money aside for a second, do you actually want to do this?"*

If yes: *"So if we can make the money work, are you in?"* Two moves only after that — move down a package, or walk politely. Never invent a number. If no, it was never the money: *"Then it's not the price, is it. What's the bit you're not sure about?"*

### "I need to think about it."

*"Course — it's your money. Can I ask what specifically you want to think about, though? Because usually it's one thing, and I'd rather answer it now than have you sitting on it."* If they name it, handle it. If they cannot name it, it is a polite no: *"If it's a no, a no is genuinely fine. Which is it?"*

### "I need to ask my partner." / "I need to run it past my wife."

Never treat this as a stall. It is usually true, and pushing on it loses the deal twice. *"Of course, that's the right thing to do. What do you reckon they'll ask you that you won't have an answer to?"*

Then: *"Want me to get on a fifteen-minute call with both of you instead? Saves you having to sell it for me."* Book the date in cal.com before hanging up.

### "We tried SEO before and it did nothing."

The most common one on a search call and the most winnable. *"I believe you, and that's most people's first go. Can I ask two things — how long did you give it, and did you ever get told what was actually being done each month?"*

The answer is almost always four months and no updates. Then: *"So you didn't find out that search doesn't work. You found out that paying someone who never showed you anything doesn't work. Fair difference?"*

### "How long before I see anything?"

Do not soften it — the honest answer sells better than the hopeful one. *"First movement in a few weeks on things you're already close on. Real change is month three to month six, and I'd rather tell you that now than pretend it's next Tuesday."*

Then: *"The money back guarantee on Growth is there for exactly that reason. We agree a clear target in writing before you start, and if I have not hit it within 180 days, you choose: your money back, or I keep working for free until we hit it. Does that timeline still work for what you're trying to do?"*

### "Can you guarantee me page one?"

Say no. Saying no here wins more calls than any yes. *"No. Google's own words are that nobody can guarantee a number one ranking, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something."*

Then say what is actually promised: *"What's in writing is a clear target we agree together before you start, on Growth or Premium. If I have not hit it within 180 days, you choose: everything you paid back, or I keep working for free until we hit it. That's a promise I can keep. Does that land differently?"*

### "We've already got someone doing it."

Never run their current agency down — `facts.md` bans saying anything critical about their staff and the same restraint applies here. *"Good, then you're not starting from nothing. What do you get from them each month, and can you tell me what moved last month?"*

If they cannot answer, leave the silence. Then: *"That's the bit I'd change. Would you want me to look at what they've built so far before you decide anything?"*

### "We're too busy right now."

*"That's a good problem and I'm not going to argue you out of it. Busy now — is that busy for the next two months, or busy for the next two years?"*

Then: *"Because this takes three to six months to bite. If we start when you're quiet you've already lost the quiet season. Would you rather have it running in the background, or genuinely park it until [date]?"* If they say park it, park it — book the date and mean it.

### "Just send me a proposal."

*"I can, and I will if you want it. But I've already got everything on the screen in front of me — can I show you the two minutes that matter instead, and then send it anyway?"*

Almost always yes. If they hold: *"No problem. What would you want to see in it that we haven't covered?"* Then send it, and book the follow-up as a date in cal.com, never a link.

### "What makes you different from the twenty other agencies emailing me?"

The honest version, not the pitch version. *"Genuinely, probably nothing you can tell from an email. What I can tell you is we do three things only — search, ads, email — and there's no long contract, no time limit, so if I'm rubbish you leave."*

Then: *"So the real question is what went wrong with the last one. What did they do that made you stop trusting agencies?"*

### "Why are you cheaper than the last quote I had?"

Never claim the other quote was a rip-off. Answer the cost question straight. *"We're in Dubai with a team of just over thirty, so what it costs me to do the work is different from what it costs a London agency to do it. Same work, different overhead."*

Then: *"The other thing is I don't spend six weeks on strategy documents before anything happens. Was your quote higher because there was more in it, or because they were more expensive?"* Only if they push again does the Clutch comparison tab get opened.

### "That's more than I expected."

Do not drop the price and do not defend it with a speech. Set it against their own numbers: *"Fair. You told me a [job] is worth about [their number]. [The Growth price: eighteen hundred, or the price they were already quoted] is about [fraction] of one of those a month. And on Growth, if we have not hit the target we agree in writing within 180 days, you choose: it all back, or we keep working for free until we hit it. Which part feels too much, the monthly number or the risk?"* If the monthly number is the problem, offer Starter honestly, and say it has no guarantee.

### "Will this get me into ChatGPT or Google's AI answers?"

*"Nobody can promise that, Google included, and I won't. What I can do is the work those tools draw on: clear answers on your site, your Google profile and reviews, and mentions on sites people trust. Google says its AI Overviews reach over two billion people a month, so it's worth doing properly."*

### "You're in Dubai. I'm in the States."

*"Fair, and it's the thing I'd worry about too. There's a four-hour overlap most days, you get a Slack channel with actual people in it, and you get my personal number — not a ticket system."*

Then: *"And there's a written update every Friday with three things in it, so you're never waiting on a time zone to find out what happened. What's the worst experience you've had with that?"*

## 3. When it goes wrong

Seven. Say the line, and do not apologise twice.

**His mind goes blank.** Stop, do not fill it. Buy the time out loud so it reads as considered rather than lost: *"Give me a second, I want to give you a proper answer to that rather than the quick one."* Then look at the notes. If it is still gone: *"Take me back a step — what was the last thing you said before that?"* They restate it and hand him the thread back.

**They will not stop talking.** They are not being difficult, they have not been asked about their business in a long time. *"Sorry, can I stop you there one second — I want to get this bit written down properly. You said [their words]. Is that the main one, or is there a bigger one behind it?"* The interruption is forgiven because it is in service of listening to them. Then close the loop: *"Right, I've got about eleven minutes left and there's one thing I really want you to see. Can I show you that and come back to this?"*

**They are visibly bored.** Do not push harder or talk faster. Name it: *"I've lost you a bit, haven't I. Let me skip this — what's the thing you actually wanted out of this call?"* Then go straight there. `mechanics.md` says the clearest cause of a lost call is eighteen minutes of monologue; boredom is that happening in real time.

**A second person joins unannounced.** Stop the pitch, reset for them, and never make the first person explain who they are. *"Hello — I'm Farhan. Good timing, we're about ten minutes in. Do you want me to do the thirty-second version of what we've covered so we're all in the same place?"* Then the question that decides the rest of the call: *"And are you two deciding this together?"* If yes and the price has not been said, this is now a two-decision-maker call — run it as one.

**He does not know the answer to a technical question.** Never bluff; `mechanics.md` bans it and a prospect can smell it. *"I don't know. I'm not going to guess at it either — I'll find out and have it to you today."* Write it down where they can see him write it, and send it the same day. The follow-through is worth more than the answer was.

**The screen share dies.** *"Screen's given up. Bear with me ten seconds — and if it doesn't come back I'll just tell you, it's not that complicated."* If it does not come back in ten seconds, do not spend the call fixing it: *"Right, I'll describe it and send you the link the second we're off. Open it while I talk if you like, it's [url]."*

**The audio dies.** Type it in the chat immediately rather than waiting to be heard — *"lost your audio my end. calling you on [number] now"* — then call the phone. When it comes back: *"Sorry about that, genuinely not your end."* Never let it die in silence, because a dropped call reads as a lost prospect.

## 4. After the call

All three go out the same day, within two hours, and all three are written the way Farhan writes email: **all lowercase, no greeting, no first name, no sign-off, two sentences a paragraph, twenty-five to eighty words.** The rules are in `/root/.claude/skills/synced/mavix-sales-sequences/references/voice.md` and they are not optional. Run every draft through `scripts/check.py` in that skill, then the humanizer, before it goes.

**If it was a yes.** Nothing in `mavix-sales-sequences` covers a closed deal, so this one is fresh. **No contract is signed on the call** — the deposit holds the slot, the onboarding link goes today, the rest lands that week.

```
good to talk. onboarding link is below, takes about five minutes.

[link]

that plus the fifty to hold the slot and we start monday. anything you want changed before then just say.
```

Ask for the deposit **once**. If they hesitate, `facts.md` has the line: *"No problem — onboarding link goes over today, sort the payment when you're ready this week."*

`[ASK FARHAN]` — whether the deposit is refundable if they change their mind before onboarding. It is not in `facts.md` and it does get asked.

**If it was a maybe. Do not write a new email.** It already exists as **situation 5, day 0 plus two hours, the recap** in `/root/.claude/skills/synced/mavix-sales-sequences/references/situations.md`. Four beats in order: the problem you both agreed on, the solution you both agreed on, the next step, who does what. Short, and accurate to what was actually said, because it is the document the deal gets measured against later.

The rest of situation 5 then runs itself — follow-up 2 on day 2 answering the one concern they actually raised, follow-up 3 on day 4 with proof matched to that concern, follow-up 4 on day 6 asking plainly for a decision, follow-up 5 on day 9 making yes, no or later equally easy. Example 5 in that skill's `references/examples.md` is the register to match. If a date was booked in cal.com on the call, the recap still goes; the date does not replace it.

**If it was a no.** Fresh, because that skill's "not interested" branch is about cold email and gets no reply at all. A no after a call someone gave you half an hour of does get one.

```
no problem at all and thanks for being straight about it.

one thing before i go. [the tip, one sentence, specific to them]. takes about twenty minutes and you do not need anyone to do it for you.

if it changes in six months you know where i am.
```

The tip is not a hook. It is mechanic ten in `mechanics.md` — give away something they can do themselves and the trust outlasts the call. It is why people come back a year later.

### The five things to log about every call

1. What a job is worth to them, and how many they close in ten.
2. The real problem **in their words** — the correction they made during the recap, not the first answer.
3. Their answer to qualifying question four, word for word.
4. The objection that actually stopped it, not the first one raised.
5. What he promised to send, and whether it went.

### The five numbers to track monthly

1. Calls booked.
2. Calls that showed up.
3. Calls that closed.
4. Average monthly fee of what closed.
5. How many of last quarter's clients are still paying at month three.

Number five matters most when there is no long contract, and it is the one everybody forgets to count.

## 5. Disqualifying, warmly

Ending a call early is a win. `mechanics.md` bans closing a business that has told you it cannot make rent, and the agenda already promised this at minute four: *"if I'm not right for you I'll say so."* This is him keeping that promise. Say it kindly, say it once, and never come back with a smaller package to rescue it.

**Below the floor.** *"I'm going to be straight with you. What you've got to spend and what this costs aren't going to meet, and I'd rather tell you now than take your money and disappoint you in month two. Search starts at [the Starter price: seven-forty-nine, or the price they were already quoted] a month, and below that I genuinely can't do it properly."* Then leave them with something: *"Here's what I'd do in your position for nothing — [the tip]. Come back when the numbers change, the door's open."*

**Wrong service.** *"Honestly? Search isn't your problem. You need people through the door in the next six weeks and search doesn't do that — different conversation, different day."* If ads is the right answer, book a separate call for it. Never sell both on one call.

**Not the decision maker.** *"I don't think it's fair on you for me to price this at you, because you'd have to go and sell it to [name] on my behalf and that never works. Can we get fifteen minutes with both of you instead?"* If they cannot get that meeting, the call ends there and it ends friendly.

**A business that cannot afford it.** The moment someone says money is genuinely tight — rent, payroll, a bad quarter — the selling stops. No smaller package, no payment plan, no "what if we started at". *"Then this isn't the month for it, and I'm not going to sell you something out of a tight month. Let me give you the two things I'd fix myself for free, and you call me when it's easier."* Then actually give them, and end the call.

The test on every line in this file is whether Farhan would be happy with the prospect hearing a recording of it six months later. This is the section where that test does the most work.
