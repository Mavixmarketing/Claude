# The trust sections

**Where they sit in the SEO audit (October 2026 order):** What we know (section 02),
What we will work on (08), Who we are (10), How we work together (16) and the close page,
Your plan, and how to start (17). The audit carries no prices anywhere. The "what usually goes wrong with agencies" page and the separate
"after you say yes" page were cut: in the audits that went out they repeated the same promises
four or five times and pushed the ask to page 22 and 29. Their content lives on as described
under each heading below.

Sections that answer the questions a prospect has in their head but does not ask out
loud. They exist so Farhan never has to explain any of this on a call.

Everything in `COPY_RULES.md` applies here. Zero em dashes, zero en dashes, straight quotes
only, no banned words, no rule-of-three lists.

**The rule that outranks everything else in this file: never write something Mavix cannot
do.** A proposal that promises a shared Drive, a same-day reply or a handover pack and then
does not deliver one is worse than a proposal that stayed quiet. If a detail below is not
true for this client, cut the line. Do not soften it and keep it.

## Section: What we know about your business

**Where it goes:** section 02, straight after the one-page short version, before "where you stand".
This is the first thing they read, and it decides whether they believe the rest.

**Why it works:** every agency proposal opens by talking about the agency. This one opens by
proving somebody listened. It costs one page and it removes the whole "they do not
understand my business" objection before it forms.

**Build it as:** a short lead paragraph, then a `table` of eight rows, then one `alert` box
holding the assumptions.

| Row | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Business | Company name, and what they actually are in one line, not their tagline |
| Industry | The real category, the way they would say it |
| What you sell | The main offer, and the average order or job value if it is known |
| Who buys it | The actual buyer. Not "homeowners", but "homeowners in Wichita with a roof over 15 years old" |
| Where you sell | City, region, country, or online only. Name the radius if it is a local business |
| Your goal | In their own words, and with their own number if they gave one |
| What is in the way | The two or three things stopping them. Their problems, not a list of services they lack |
| The biggest opening | The single largest thing available to them. One sentence. This is the hook for the rest of the document |

**Quote them wherever possible.** If the prospect said "I need 100 leads a month and I have a
thousand dollars", the goal row says that, in those words. Recognising their own sentence in
print is what makes the page land.

**Then the assumptions box, and it is not optional.** Label it honestly, for example:

> **Where we are guessing.** You have not told us your average job value yet, so we have used
> 6,500 USD, which is typical for roof replacement in Kansas. If that number is wrong, several
> figures later in this document move with it. Tell us the real one and we will redo them.

Most agencies pretend to know. Saying plainly what was assumed, and inviting a correction, is
the single most trust-building paragraph in the whole document. Use it every time. If nothing
was assumed, say that instead and move on.

**Never invent a fact and present it as something they told you.** If it was not said, it
goes in the assumptions box or it does not appear.

## Section: Who we are

**Where it goes:** unchanged, after the scope and the numbers. By that point the document has
earned the right to talk about Mavix.

**Banned opening, and anything like it:**

> We are a full service digital marketing agency passionate about helping businesses grow.

That sentence means nothing and every prospect has read it fifty times. Delete it on sight.

**Answer five questions instead, in this order, one short paragraph each:**

1. **Who we are.** Use the company story below. Based in Dubai, a team of 30+ people
   employed by Mavix, one named account manager on every account, and Mohd Farhan, the
   founder, on the first call and in every monthly review. That answers the loudest fear a
   prospect has: being handed to someone who does not know them.
2. **What we do, specifically.** SEO, paid ads (Google and Meta) and email. Name the actual
   disciplines, not "full service". If a service is not on the list, say so.
3. **Who we work with.** The kinds of businesses Mavix actually serves. Small and mid sized
   companies who need the phone to ring, not brands with a media department. Say the size of
   business honestly, because a prospect who is too big or too small should know now.
4. **What we fix.** Money going into ads with nothing coming back. Tracking that was never
   set up properly. A site that ranks for nothing that sells. Rankings that never turned into
   customers. Frame it as problems, not as service names.
5. **Why Mavix exists.** The honest reason, in Farhan's words. If he has not given one, ask
   him rather than writing a founding myth.

Keep the Google Partner block, the credential circles and the client logo wall exactly as they
are. Those are the proof. The words above are the context.

Do not claim anything beyond the company story: no client count, no award, no office or
founding story it does not name. An unverifiable number here poisons a document whose whole job
is trust. Never write "small team", "Farhan runs your account himself" or "there is nobody behind
him": they contradict the call kit and the website.

### The company story (October 2026)

Facts from mavixmarketing.com (about page and home page, read 23 October 2026) plus Farhan's
confirmation of Dubai and 30+ people. Shorten to fit; never add to it.

> Mavix Marketing is based in Dubai, with a team of 30+ people across Dubai, Los Angeles and
> New Delhi. Everyone who does the work is employed by Mavix, not subcontracted. We do SEO, paid
> ads and email, and have done since 2019. On search we are not a traditional SEO agency: we get
> businesses found on Google, Google Maps, and in AI answers such as Google's AI Overviews and
> ChatGPT. Every account has one named account manager for the day to day. Our founder, Mohd
> Farhan, takes the first call with every new client and joins every monthly review. Reports
> lead with enquiries, and every number comes from the client's own accounts.

## Section: What we will work on (was "Exactly what you get")

**Where it goes:** section 08, the scope page.

**The seven work areas come first** (`work_areas`, see SKILL.md Step 3b): their website,
Google rankings and keyword research, blogs and helpful content, links and PR, Google Maps and
reviews, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Each row says
what we do and what it means for them, in plain words. Then two short things that matter as
much as the rows. No prices on this page.

**1. What is not included.** Say it plainly, in its own short list. This is the single best
defence against the complaint that agencies spring costs later.

- Ad budget. It is paid by the client, in the client's own account, and it never passes
  through Mavix.
- Publisher and placement fees on SEO, paid by the client directly, at no markup. No number
  in the audit: the usual range goes with the price, after the yes.
- A website rebuild, unless it is written into the scope.
- Photography and video production, unless it is written into the scope.
- Software the client already pays for, such as a CRM or an email platform.
- Anything outside the four groups, which gets quoted before it is started, never after.

**2. Who does what.** A short two column block. What Mavix does, and the small number of
things that need the client. Access, approvals, and answers to questions. Being specific about
the client's side makes the whole document read as a working plan rather than a sales
document.

**On turnaround times.** Only state a response time Farhan has actually agreed to. If he has,
write it exactly. If he has not, use this and nothing stronger: "We answer on the same working
day. Anything larger than a quick change gets a date rather than a maybe." Never promise
24 hour turnaround, dedicated hours, or an availability window unless it is true.

## Section: What usually goes wrong with agencies, and how we handle it

**Where it goes in the SEO proposal: not as its own page any more.** Use the rows below as
the source for FAQ answers (section 15) and for the lines on the How we work together page.
If the prospect described a bad agency experience, answer that row as one of the eight FAQs,
in their words. The full table is kept here for the call kit and for other documents.

**Why it works:** most prospects have been burned before, and the ones who have not have heard
the stories. Naming the failure out loud, before they raise it, is disarming. It also quietly
says that Mavix knows what bad looks like.

**Build it as:** a short lead, then a `table` with two columns, ten rows, then one `neon`
line to close. Keep every cell to one or two lines. Ten tight rows fit a page. Twelve do not.

| What usually goes wrong | How this works instead |
|---|---|
| You meet someone impressive, sign, and then get handed to a junior you never met | One named account manager from day one, and the founder, Mohd Farhan, joins every monthly review |
| You cannot get hold of anyone when you need them | One channel, agreed on day one, and an answer on the same working day |
| Everything is "we are working on it" and you never see what that means | A written plan with named deliverables, and a monthly report showing what was actually done |
| Reports full of impressions, clicks and followers, none of which pay wages | We report leads, cost per lead, sales and return. Traffic numbers are context, never the headline |
| No strategy, just a stream of tasks | Strategy first, then execution, then measurement, then change what the numbers say to change |
| The same plan every client gets, with the name swapped | The "what we know" page is the research. It exists because the plan comes after the homework |
| Costs appear that were never mentioned | What is included and what is not, both written down, in the scope section |
| A long contract, and nothing to show for the first three months | No long contract, no time limit, no penalty for leaving |
| The agency owns the ad account and you lose everything if you go | Every account is yours, in your name, from day one. We hold access, not ownership |
| Promises of page one, or a fixed number of leads, that nobody can keep | We tell you what we control and what we do not, and we put a guarantee behind the part we control |

**Adapt the wording to the prospect.** If they have already described a bad agency experience,
put that row first and use their words in it. If they have never worked with an agency, keep
the rows but soften the lead so it does not read as a warning about a thing they have not
experienced.

**Close with one honest line, not a boast.** For example:

> None of this makes us special. It makes us normal. The reason it is worth writing down is
> that so many agencies do not do it.

## Section: How we work together

**Where it goes:** straight after the agency problems section. One answers the fear, this one
shows the machinery.

**Build it as:** three blocks on one page. A small `table` for the channels and reporting, a
`two_col` for ownership, and an `alert` for the exit. Reporting is a row in the channels table
(one written report a month plus a 30 minute call, and the live dashboard), not its own page.

### Talking to each other

| For | We use |
|---|---|
| Day to day | WhatsApp, one group, set up in the first 48 hours |
| Calls and reviews | Google Meet |
| Documents, reports and files | A shared Google Drive folder, yours to keep |
| Reporting | One written report every month, plus a call to go through it |
| Anything urgent | The same WhatsApp group. There is no ticket queue to get lost in |

Then one line naming who they deal with: their named account manager (by name once one is
assigned), with Mohd Farhan in every monthly review. And the response commitment
from the scope section, repeated here in one sentence.

### Who owns what

Say it flatly, because this is where most trust is won:

> Every account stays in your name. Google Ads, Meta Business Manager, Google Analytics,
> Search Console, Tag Manager, your website, your CRM and your email platform belong to you.
> Mavix is given the access needed to do the work, and nothing more. If an account has to be
> created from scratch, it is created in your name, not ours.

### If you decide to stop

Do not hide the exit. An agency that makes leaving hard is telling you something.

- No long contract. The agreement has no time limit and only ties us, to deliver. No penalty, no cancellation fee.
- Every account stays yours, along with everything inside it.
- Your data, campaigns, content and reporting history stay where they are.
- You can remove our access whenever you want, without asking.
- You get a final report and whatever we built, handed over properly.

**Check this against the actual contract before it goes out.** If the signed agreement says
something different about notice or handover, the contract wins and this page changes to
match it. The two documents must never disagree.

## Section: Your plan, and how to start (the close page)

**Where it goes:** the final page, section 17. It replaces the old "after you say yes" page,
the old final CTA block and, since October 2026, the price.

**Why it works:** the last thing standing between a prospect and a yes is usually not price.
It is not knowing exactly what they are saying yes to, and what happens tomorrow. The October
audits ended with "Reply with a plan", which made the prospect go back, compare three plans
and decide alone. This page decides for them and makes the next step one tap. The price is
personal, so it comes after the yes.

**Build it with `close_page`.** It holds four things and nothing else:

1. **The recommended plan of work** for this prospect: the plan name, one line on what it is
   for them, three to five "every month" lines, and the guarantee in one line. **No price.**
2. **The 90-day target we would agree**, in the same words as the targets page, and how it is
   checked.
3. **The first three things we do**, specific to this prospect, each one line.
4. **Two ways to move forward:** reply "yes" to the email and we send the plan and their
   personalised price, or book a 15-minute walkthrough at
   https://cal.id/mavixmarketing/strategy-call.

**On the first three things.** Only list work that really happens in the first weeks. If a
fix needs the client's access first, it still counts, but the timing on the targets and plan
pages must allow for it. A missed first promise is worse than a slower one.

**Do not promise a welcome pack, an onboarding form or a 48 hour timetable** on this page
unless Farhan has confirmed it exists. If he wants it in, it has to be built before the page is
sent to anyone.
