# The call script

Plain markdown. No design. Packed with everything, in this order.

The reasoning behind every move below — which mechanics actually work, which failure modes lose calls, and what is banned — is in `references/mechanics.md`. Read it once before writing a script.

## The header, before any script

1. **Ground rules** — the call format, pricing, floor, capacity, terms, closing, tone. Lifted from `facts.md`, trimmed to this prospect.
2. **The prospect in ten lines** — who they are, who owns it, how long, what is excellent about them, what is broken. Plus a "do not say" line for anything the research found that is wrong or unverified.
3. **Tabs to open** — one numbered list of real clickable URLs in call order. Then a short "how to use them" note: which pairs go side by side, which one stings, which stays shut unless they push on price.
4. **Check yourself before you dial** — the four or five things research could not reach. Their phone on mobile. Whether a new review has landed. Whether the map box has moved. His real client count this month.
5. **The findings** — every verified number, in tables. The headline comparison first.

## Then the script, minute by minute

### The open (0-4)

Warm up properly. Never walk in and start selling.

Check-in → camera → then the light one: *"Is this your first call of the day, or have you already had ten?"* → then take the pressure out: *"I'll try to make this one less painful than other agency calls you've sat through. There's no forty-seven slide presentation coming, I promise."*

Then **genuine curiosity, built from the research** — how they got started, an odd combination in their background, something a review mentioned. Let it run a minute or two. Then location if it needs another beat.

Then **the intro from `facts.md`, word for word** — including the right closing paragraph for the service and package. There are three versions and using the wrong one puts a search guarantee on an ads call.

Then homework and the decision maker: *"I spent about half an hour on your setup yesterday. I found a few things worth your time — one of them is free to fix and it'll probably annoy you. Quick one though — is this you deciding, or is [partner] in on this too?"*

If a second decision maker is not on the call: run everything, **say no price**, book the next one with both.

### The agenda (4)

What happens in the next half hour, then *"and if I'm not right for you I'll say so — I'd rather tell you today than take your money and let you down in month three. Sound fair?"* Wait for an answer.

### Discovery (4-13)

Eight questions. They talk, he types, three seconds of silence after every answer.

What they do · **what a job is worth** · how many a month · where customers come from · enquiries and close rate · where they want to be · **why they replied to the email** · what they tried before.

Then going one layer deeper, two or three times: *"Say more about that?"* → *"What's that actually costing you?"* → *"And if it's the same this time next year, where does that leave you?"*

Then **the four qualifying questions**, written out one at a time — money, who actually decides, timing, and what matters to them when they pick someone. The fourth answer gets reused word for word when the offer is described.

Then set the ending: *"At the end there's three things you can say. Yes. No — and a no is completely fine. Or 'let me think about it', which we both know is usually a polite no. Can we keep it to a yes or a no?"*

### Play it back, then diagnose (13-16)

Recap their numbers and their words, ask *"have I missed anything?"*, take the correction — the correction is usually the real problem.

Then diagnose, carefully: it is not a criticism of their work. Their work is better than the competition's. The problem is they are not in the room at the moment somebody decides to spend.

Then ask permission: *"Want me to show you?"*

### Show them the money (16-21)

Two or three findings. **Ask before telling** every time — *"when do you reckon your last review came in?"* lands ten times harder than announcing it.

**Every finding ends in money**, using the job value they gave in discovery.

Include what to say if a finding turns out to be wrong (*"That's on me. Good — one less thing."*) and what to say if the audit finds nothing bad.

### What we'd do (21-24)

The plan in plain words. **The timeline as a table** — week 1, weeks 2-4, month 2, month 3, months 4-6. Honest about how long rankings take.

**What they get weekly and monthly**, specifically: a written update every Friday with three things in it, a one-page monthly report including what did not move, a Slack channel, and his personal number.

**The agency-problems table** — what goes wrong everywhere else against what happens here. Seven rows: no updates, different answers from different people, hidden charges, AI content, forty-page reports, time zones, disappearing after signature.

**The offer as five answers:** what are we doing, how, what do you get, how long, what does it cost.

Then risk removed: no setup fee, no contract, 30 days, accounts in their name, the guarantee.

**Optional: the hiring comparison.** Salary, insurance, payroll, recruiting, training, and the risk they leave — against a whole team with 30 days' notice. Qualitative, no invented salary figures.

### Trial close, price, ask (24-26)

*"Based on everything we've talked about — does this sound like the kind of thing you're looking for?"* **Never price into a maybe.**

Then the three packages, Growth recommended, said slowly. **Then stop and count to ten.**

Then set the price against one job of theirs. Then the Clutch comparison only if they flinch.

Then *"So — do you want to get going?"* and silence.

If money is the only blocker: *"Money aside for a second — do you actually want to do this?"* then *"So if we can make the money work, are you in?"*

### The close (26-30)

**Yes** — the deposit ask, once, then drop it if they hesitate. Onboarding link same day. *"What else do you want me to show you?"* — never *"any questions?"*

**Maybe** — a date and time booked in cal.com before hanging up. Not a link.

**No** — *"was it the price, the timing, or just not for you?"* then one genuinely useful free tip on the way out. It costs nothing and it is why people come back.

**Disqualify** — the exact words to end a call early, warmly.

## Then the back sections

All four of these are written out in full in `references/playbook.md`. Copy them in and tailor, never improvise them live.

- **Objections** — the twelve in `playbook.md`, plus for an ads kit the six in `ads-track.md` section 6. Add any this specific prospect is likely to raise.
- **The four qualifying questions** — word for word in `playbook.md`.
- **When it goes wrong** — the seven scenarios in `playbook.md`.
- **Never say** — the list, and the scope line for "can you also do…"
- **After the call** — the yes, maybe and no messages in `playbook.md`. Five questions to log. Five numbers to track monthly.
- **Disqualifying warmly** — the exact words, in `playbook.md`.
- **How to get this data next time** — the tools and what they cost.
- **Sources** — every tool, every date, and the line "no figure in this document was invented".
