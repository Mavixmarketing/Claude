# Copy rules: the humanizer

Load this BEFORE writing the first sentence. Applying it as a cleanup pass does not work,
because by then the draft already has the shape.

## Scope: what these rules apply to

**Prose only.** Every sentence a prospect reads: section leads, paragraphs, bullets, table
cells, case-study narrative, FAQ answers, chart notes, the outreach email.

**These rules do NOT apply to:**

- Section kicker labels (`THE SITUATION`, `WHAT CLIENTS SAY`) which are uppercase by design
- Two-part headlines with a blue accent, which are the house style
- Stat card labels, table headers, scorecard labels, pricing tier names
- Chart axis labels and legends
- Product and service names (see exemptions)

Without this carve-out the humanizer fights the design system. The design spec wins on
labels; this file wins on sentences.

## Exemptions: never rename these

These words appear on the banned list but are correct in context. Skip them when they are
part of a product, platform or service name:

`optimisation` / `optimize` (AI Optimize SEO, GMB optimisation, bid optimisation) ·
`dynamic` (Dynamic Search Ads, dynamic retargeting, dynamic creative) ·
`comprehensive` (only inside a quoted plan name) · `performance` (Performance Max) ·
`conversions` (Conversions API) · `audience` · `impressions` · `attribution`

Everywhere else in prose, the ban stands.

## The highest-signal tells, ranked

| Tell | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Em dashes and en dashes | The most reliable single giveaway |
| "In today's fast-paced world" and every variant | Roughly 107x more common in AI text |
| "delve" | The largest single spike of any tracked word |
| "Here's the thing" | Found in about a third of sampled AI posts |
| One sentence per paragraph, all the way down | 91% of sampled AI social posts |
| Uniform sentence length | Low variance is what detectors actually score |
| Copula avoidance ("serves as", "stands as", "represents a") | Sharply reduced plain "is" and "are" |

If you fix only three things: cut every dash, vary sentence length, delete the opening cliche.

## Banned vocabulary

**Verbs.** delve, embark, navigate (figurative), leverage, harness, utilise, facilitate,
streamline, underscore, illuminate, shed light on, bolster, unlock, elevate, transform,
innovate, showcase, empower, foster, cultivate, garner, align with, endeavour, entail, underpin

**Adjectives.** pivotal, robust, crucial, key (as adjective), seamless, cutting-edge,
revolutionary, groundbreaking, transformative, innovative, comprehensive, holistic, nuanced,
multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, invaluable, integral, dynamic, powerful, renowned,
vibrant, whimsical, unparalleled, state-of-the-art, industry-leading, best-in-class,
next-generation

**Nouns.** landscape (abstract), ecosystem (non-technical), realm, tapestry, symphony, odyssey,
voyage, journey (figurative), paradigm, framework, synergy, spectrum, trajectory, alignment,
resonance, complexity, nuance, plethora, myriad, kaleidoscope, advent

**Adverbs.** profoundly, seamlessly, significantly, relentlessly, meticulously, notably,
particularly, ultimately, arguably, preemptively

**Replacements.** leverage to use. facilitate to help. utilise to use. delve into to explore,
or just start. underscore to show or prove. bolster to support. plethora to a lot. myriad to
many. paradigm to approach. pivotal to key, or delete. robust to strong, or delete.
comprehensive to full, or delete. Most of these delete cleanly.

## Banned sentence patterns

- **Rule of three.** "fast, efficient and reliable." Vary the count.
- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not X, it's Y." "Not only X but also Y." Say the positive claim.
- **Stop X, start Y.**
- **Setup-payoff pairs** repeated with the same shape paragraph after paragraph.
- **Hedge chains.** "It's worth noting that in many cases it may be beneficial to consider..."
- **Copula avoidance.** "serves as", "stands as", "represents a", "boasts", "features". Use is, are, has.
- **Superficial -ing tails.** "..., highlighting the importance of X." Cut the clause.
- **False ranges.** "from the boardroom to the factory floor" where the ends sit on no real scale.
- **Over-signposting.** "First... Second... Finally..." imposed on ideas that do not need it.
- **Aphorism formulas.** "X is the language of Y."
- **Manufactured punchlines.** Four short fragments in a row to build drama.

## Banned openings

Never open a section with: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era where", "As technology
continues to evolve", "Modern X is changing everything", "I want to talk about", a heading
followed by one line restating the heading, or any sentence describing what the document is
about to do.

**Open with the finding, the number, or a specific observation.** For a competitor section,
open with what the competitors are doing, not "We analysed your competitors."

## Banned closings

Never close with "In conclusion", "Overall", "To sum up", "In summary", "Ultimately", "At the
end of the day", "As we have seen", a long recap, "The future of X is", or generic optimism.

**End on the last concrete fact or the actual ask.** A missing conclusion reads better than a
hollow one.

## Empty filler, delete on sight

It's worth mentioning · Needless to say · Without a doubt · As mentioned earlier · One thing to
keep in mind · It goes without saying · As you can imagine · It is important to note that · In
many cases · As a result of this · When it comes to · In terms of · This begs the question ·
One thing is certain

Overused transitions: Moreover · Furthermore · Additionally · Consequently · Thus · Hence ·
Therefore · That being said · At its core · To put it simply · From a broader perspective ·
A key takeaway is

Most sentences survive the deletion untouched. Where a connective is needed, "and", "but",
"so" and "also" carry it.

## Hedging

generally speaking · broadly speaking · to some extent · arguably · it could be argued that ·
it seems that · one could argue · may potentially · tends to · somewhat · rather

Say it or do not. "This may potentially help improve rankings over time" becomes "This gets you
ranking, usually inside 60 to 90 days."

## Punctuation

- **Zero em dashes and en dashes anywhere in the document or the email.** Replace with a full
  stop (best), a comma, a colon, or brackets. Catch spaced em dashes and double hyphens too.
- **Straight quotes only.** No curly or smart quotes.
- Sentence-initial "And" and "But" are normal. So are occasional fragments.
- Contractions are fine and usually better.
- Bold what must not be missed, nothing else.

## Email rules

Cold outreach only, printed in chat, never inside the PDF. Three to five lines of body.

Banned: "I hope this email finds you well" · "I am writing to" · "I wanted to reach out" ·
"I came across your profile/business" · "I am pleased to" · "Please don't hesitate to reach
out" (say "let me know") · "Best regards" / "Sincerely" / "Kind regards" · "Thank you for your
time. I look forward to your response." · "Circling back" · a PS line · restating the purpose
at the end · listing services instead of naming an outcome.

**What works:** one specific observation about them, one problem they feel, one outcome, one
low-friction ask. Sign off "Mohd Farhan" alone, no title, company, phone or link.

## What NOT to flag

Do not gut good writing chasing tells. On their own these mean nothing: clean grammar, formal
vocabulary, a mix of registers, one short punchy sentence, a single "however".

Look for **clusters.** One long sentence proves nothing. Dashes plus a rule-of-three plus
"vibrant tapestry" plus a "Conclusion" heading is a confession.

Actively preserve what proves a human wrote it: oddly specific detail, mixed or unresolved
opinions, genuine asides and self-corrections, uneven sentence rhythm.

## Write more of these

- **Specific, hard-to-fabricate detail.** A real date, a real filename, an exact figure.
- **Mixed feelings.** "This mostly works, but something about it bothers me."
- **Direct address.** "You're paying for clicks that will never buy" beats "Budget is being
  allocated to non-converting traffic."
- **Admitting a limit.** "We don't have a case study in your exact sector" is more persuasive
  than one that does not survive checking.
- **Concrete nouns over abstractions.** Name the thing.

## Sentence rhythm

AI produces sentences of near-identical length. Humans do not. Mix a long sentence that carries
a full thought through several clauses with a short one. Like that. Occasionally a fragment.
Do not start three sentences in a row with "We" or "Our".

Read the opening and the closing aloud. That is where fake tone concentrates.

## The truth rule, which outranks style

Never assert a fact, number, date or citation that was not in the source material. A
fabrication is a defect even when it reads better than the vague original.

This applies to **metrics, figures, dates and citations** without exception. Every number
carries a source or is labelled an estimate, in the chart note or beside the figure.

**Case studies have no exception.** They are the real ones published at
mavixmarketing.com/case-studies. Name the client exactly the way the website names it ("Construction
management firm", "Online home goods store", "La Vela Coffee", "My Favorite Therapists", "All Dogs
Rock"), use the website's numbers and timeframe exactly, and put the case study's URL on the page
and in the CTA. Never invent a client name, city, staff count, baseline number or execution detail
that is not on the website case study or printed on the screenshot. Where a part of the page needs
more detail than the website summary gives, fetch the live case study page and use only what it
says, or write it in general terms that are true of how Mavix works ("we rebuilt the service
pages") with no specific numbers.

## Verify before delivering

Run this on the finished PDF, not the source:

```bash
python3 - <<'EOF'
import fitz, sys
BANNED = ["leverage","robust","seamless","elevate","unlock","delve","foster","tapestry",
 "testament","cutting-edge","bespoke","synergy","meticulous","myriad","plethora","holistic",
 "empower","streamline","pivotal","game-chang","state-of-the-art","world-class","best-in-class",
 "in today's","at the end of the day","when it comes to","that being said","in conclusion",
 "rest assured","look no further","not only","it is important to note","needless to say",
 "furthermore","moreover","supercharge","transformative","revolutionary","groundbreaking",
 "drive results","move the needle","please don't hesitate","best regards","comprehensive"]
EXEMPT = ["dynamic retargeting","dynamic search ads","dynamic creative","ai optimize seo",
          "gmb optimisation","conversions api","performance max"]
d = fitz.open(sys.argv[1]); t = "".join(p.get_text() for p in d); low = t.lower()
for e in EXEMPT: low = low.replace(e, "")
hits = {"em dash": t.count("—"), "en dash": t.count("–"),
        "curly quote": sum(t.count(c) for c in "“”‘’")}
found = [b for b in BANNED if b in low]
print(hits, "banned:", found or "none")
print("PASS" if not any(hits.values()) and not found else "FAIL")
EOF
```

Then read it by eye for rule-of-three lists, duplicated paragraphs, and process-first openers.
